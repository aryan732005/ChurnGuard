ChurnGuard AI — Methodology & Research Context

This page documents the business problem, data pipeline, validation rigour, fairness checks, and ethical boundaries of this academic churn-prediction system.

Problem Statement

Customer churn — when subscribers cancel or fail to renew — is one of the most costly problems in telecommunications. Industry estimates suggest acquiring a new customer costs five to seven times more than retaining an existing one. When a telecom provider can identify at-risk customers before they leave, it can intervene with targeted retention offers, service improvements, or proactive support.

This project addresses that business problem by building a machine-learning classifier that estimates churn probability from billing, contract, and service-usage features. The goal is not perfect prediction, but actionable risk scoring that supports retention planning while remaining transparent about limitations and fairness.

Related Work / Literature Context

Churn prediction has been studied extensively in telecom and subscription businesses. Comparative studies (e.g. Vafeiadis et al., 2015) consistently find that ensemble methods — Random Forest and Gradient Boosting — perform strongly on benchmark telco datasets, often exceeding single classifiers. Logistic regression remains a common baseline because it is interpretable and handles linear relationships between contract tenure, pricing, and churn risk well.

This project follows that established pattern: we train three mainstream scikit-learn classifiers, compare them against a naive majority-class baseline, and select the best model by ROC AUC — a standard choice when classes are imbalanced and threshold selection matters.

Ethical Considerations

  • Appropriate use: This model supports exploratory retention planning and academic demonstration. It should not be the sole basis for denying service, pricing discrimination, or automated account termination.
  • Discrimination risk: Using churn scores to justify different retention offers by protected characteristics (gender, age) could reinforce unfair treatment even if the model is not explicitly trained on those attributes as primary drivers.
  • Academic scope: ChurnGuard AI is a demonstrative academic system — not a production deployment recommendation. Real deployment would require legal review, ongoing monitoring, and stakeholder governance.

Data Privacy

The Telco Customer Churn dataset used here is a public, anonymised benchmark. It contains no real customer names, addresses, or contact information — only synthetic or de-identified account identifiers. This application does not store submitted prediction inputs beyond the current browser session, and authentication credentials are stored as password hashes, not plaintext.

Limitations

  • Single-domain data: Patterns learned from one telecom context may not generalise to other industries or regions.
  • Class imbalance trade-offs: Class weighting improves recall on churners but increases false positives compared to unweighted training.
  • No external factors: Competitor pricing, network outages, and macroeconomic shifts are not in the feature set.
  • Basic fairness audit: We checked gender and senior-citizen subgroups only; a production system would need more exhaustive fairness testing.
  • Synthetic variant: If generated data is used, statistical properties approximate but do not identical-match real telco populations.

Future Work

  1. SHAP explainability: Per-prediction feature attributions so retention teams understand why a specific customer was flagged.
  2. Real-time pipelines: Integrate streaming billing and usage data instead of batch CSV snapshots.
  3. Exhaustive fairness auditing: Expand to intersectional groups, equalised-odds metrics, and bias mitigation techniques.
  4. Production monitoring: Track model drift, calibration decay, and performance degradation after deployment.

Evaluation Metrics

Accuracy

(TP + TN) / (TP + TN + FP + FN)

Overall correctness — misleading alone when ~70% of customers are retained.

Precision

TP / (TP + FP)

Of predicted churners, how many actually churned.

Recall

TP / (TP + FN)

Of actual churners, how many were caught — critical for retention outreach.

F1 Score

2 × (P × R) / (P + R)

Balance between precision and recall under imbalance.

ROC AUC

Area Under ROC Curve

Primary model-selection metric — threshold-independent discriminative ability.
